# Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025

**MINISTRY OF ELECTRONICS AND INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY (MeitY)**  
*New Delhi, 2025*  

**THE DIGITAL PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION RULES, 2025**  

In exercise of the powers conferred by section 41 of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (22 of 2023), the Central Government hereby makes the following rules:—

---

## CHAPTER I: GENERAL PROVISIONS

### Rule 1: Short title and commencement
(1) These rules may be called the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.  
(2) They shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette, save as otherwise expressly provided in these rules.

### Rule 2: Definitions
(1) In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires,—  
(a) **"Act"** means the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (22 of 2023);  
(b) **"Applicant"** means an entity seeking registration as a Consent Manager under section 6(9);  
(c) **"Data Protection Impact Assessment"** or **"DPIA"** means the formal process undertaken by a Significant Data Fiduciary under section 10(2)(c) to assess risks to Data Principals;  
(d) **"Parental Verification"** means an identity-grounded verification mechanism to ascertain the legal guardianship or parenthood of an individual prior to processing data of a child under section 9.

---

## CHAPTER II: NOTICE AND CONSENT FRAMEWORK

### Rule 3: Requirements and format of notice
(1) The notice referred to in sub-section (1) of section 5 shall be presented in clear, plain language that is easily comprehensible to an individual of ordinary prudence.  
(2) The notice shall explicitly enumerate:  
(a) itemized categories of personal data requested;  
(b) specific, unambiguous business purposes for processing;  
(c) data retention periods or criteria for determining retention;  
(d) step-by-step procedures to withdraw consent under section 6(4);  
(e) direct electronic link to the Data Protection Officer or grievance redressal officer; and  
(f) statutory URL and postal address of the Data Protection Board of India.  
(3) Notice must be made accessible across all 22 languages specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution.

### Rule 4: Consent Manager registration and technical standards
(1) An entity desiring to act as a Consent Manager shall possess an interoperable digital architecture compliant with ISO/IEC 27001 or equivalent standards notified by the Board.  
(2) Consent Managers must be incorporated in India, maintain capital adequacy of at least ₹5 crore, and employ end-to-end cryptographic audit trails for consent lifecycle logs.  
(3) A Consent Manager shall not store, monetize, or access the underlying personal data processed by the Data Fiduciary, operating exclusively as a consent conduit.

---

## CHAPTER III: SPECIAL PROTECTIONS FOR CHILDREN

### Rule 5: Verifiable parental consent
(1) For the purposes of section 9(1), a Data Fiduciary shall deploy verifiable parental consent mechanisms using:  
(a) government-issued digital identity verification (e.g., DigiLocker, Aadhaar-based OTP verification where authorized); or  
(b) banking or tokenized digital payment verification of nominal value refunded instantly.  
(2) A Data Fiduciary shall not condition a child's access to educational, health, or welfare services on unnecessary personal data collection.

### Rule 6: Prohibition of tracking and behavioural profiling
(1) In accordance with section 9(3), no Data Fiduciary shall employ web cookies, device fingerprinting, machine learning models, or behavioural analytics to track or profile children under 18 years of age.  
(2) Targeted advertisements directed towards children based on browsing history or profiling algorithms are strictly forbidden.

---

## CHAPTER IV: SIGNIFICANT DATA FIDUCIARIES (SDF)

### Rule 7: Criteria for designation as Significant Data Fiduciary
The Central Government shall notify an entity as a Significant Data Fiduciary under section 10 if it satisfies one or more of the following thresholds:  
(a) processing personal data of more than 5 million Data Principals within any continuous 12-month period;  
(b) processing biometric, genomic, or precise financial data on a systematic scale;  
(c) providing online algorithmic recommendation engines impacting public discourse or democratic elections; or  
(d) operating critical national infrastructure services.

### Rule 8: Obligations of Data Protection Officer (DPO)
(1) The DPO appointed under section 10(2)(a) must be an Indian resident with at least 5 years of experience in data privacy, law, or information security.  
(2) The DPO shall act as the primary liaison between the SDF, Data Principals, and the Board, and shall be shielded from retaliatory dismissal for performing statutory duties.

### Rule 9: Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) and Audit
(1) Every SDF shall conduct an annual DPIA before introducing new high-risk automated decision-making technologies or large-scale data sharing.  
(2) An independent data auditor certified by CERT-In or ICAI shall verify SDF compliance annually and submit an audit report to the Board.

---

## CHAPTER V: GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL AND INCIDENT REPORTING

### Rule 10: Timelines for grievance redressal
(1) Every Data Fiduciary shall acknowledge receipt of a grievance filed under section 13 within 24 hours.  
(2) The Data Fiduciary shall dispose of and communicate its final decision on the grievance within 72 hours, not exceeding a maximum of 7 business days under exceptional circumstances.

### Rule 11: Personal data breach notification protocol
(1) For the purposes of section 8(6), a Data Fiduciary shall notify the Board within 6 hours of detecting a personal data breach compromising integrity, confidentiality, or availability.  
(2) The notification to affected Data Principals shall contain:  
(a) description of the nature and scope of the breach;  
(b) anticipated consequences and potential harm; and  
(c) immediate protective actions recommended for the Data Principal.

---

## CHAPTER VI: CROSS-BORDER TRANSFERS

### Rule 12: Restricted territories (Negative List)
(1) Cross-border transfer of personal data is permitted to all jurisdictions except those specifically notified by the Central Government on a negative list under section 16(1).  
(2) Any transfer of personal data involving sensitive government datasets or critical personal data shall require prior approval from the Central Government.
